Milestone M1 Project Foundation

Project Overview

BeaconX addresses the infrastructure burden of monitoring blockchain events by offering shared, production-grade observers through NFT-based subscriptions.

Problem

Teams running blockchain applications need reliable, real-time access to on-chain events. Building this infrastructure requires maintaining redundant observers, proxies, message queues, and replay buffers—all of which translate to significant operational costs and complexity.

Solution

BeaconX shares our battle-tested observer infrastructure through NFT-gated subscriptions. Each NFT represents a dedicated event queue with automatic entitlement management, replay capabilities, and production-grade resilience.

Customer Discovery & Validation

Executive Summary

Through systematic customer discovery interviews with blockchain developers and infrastructure teams, we validated that BeaconX addresses a critical infrastructure pain point. The discovery process confirmed that development teams face substantial costs and operational complexity when maintaining production-grade blockchain event observers. Our NFT-based subscription model successfully addresses these challenges while providing automated entitlement management and reliable replay capabilities.

Problem Validated

High infrastructure costs and operational burden of running redundant observer systems for blockchain event monitoring

Solution Hypothesis

NFT-gated access to shared observer infrastructure with automatic entitlement tracking and message replay

Validation Outcome

Confirmed demand with active users deploying to production and requesting expanded network coverage

1. Problem Identification

Problem Statement

Blockchain development teams require real-time, reliable access to on-chain events to build responsive applications. However, maintaining production-grade observer infrastructure presents significant challenges:

  • Infrastructure Redundancy: Teams must run multiple observer instances, backup servers, and failover systems to ensure high availability, leading to duplicated infrastructure costs.
  • Operational Complexity: Managing custom proxy layers, message queues, and replay buffers requires specialized expertise and ongoing maintenance.
  • Historical Data Requirements: Deep history access for replay and debugging scenarios can require maintaining databases exceeding 20TB in size.
  • Resource Allocation Trade-offs: Smaller teams must choose between dedicating development resources to core product features or maintaining infrastructure reliability.
Discovery Context

This problem emerged organically through our own experience building and maintaining blockchain observer infrastructure across multiple production projects. We encountered the same pain points our target customers face: high hosting costs, operational complexity, and the challenge of maintaining high availability without sacrificing development velocity. This firsthand experience informed our hypothesis that a shared infrastructure model could significantly reduce these burdens while maintaining production-grade reliability.

2. Solution Hypothesis

BeaconX proposes a novel approach to blockchain event infrastructure by combining three key components:

1. Shared Infrastructure Model

Rather than each team maintaining their own observer infrastructure, BeaconX operates production-grade observers with built-in redundancy, failover capabilities, and deep historical data access. This shared model eliminates infrastructure duplication while maintaining isolation between customers through dedicated message queues.

2. NFT-Based Entitlement

Access control is managed through subscription NFTs that represent queue ownership. Each NFT encapsulates stream access rights, subscription duration, and replay retention policies. When NFTs are transferred or expire, our backend automatically updates permissions without manual intervention, providing transparent on-chain audit trails.

3. Automated Queue Management

Our backend continuously monitors NFT ownership and subscription status, automatically provisioning or decommissioning event queues as needed. This eliminates manual credential rotation and provides teams with instant access to historical message replay after client disconnections.

4. Production-Grade Reliability

The infrastructure includes redundant observers, automatic failover, message persistence, and rate-limited public API endpoints. We maintain the complexity of high-availability systems so customers can focus on building their core products.

Value Proposition

This approach allows development teams to access enterprise-grade blockchain event infrastructure at a fraction of the cost of building and maintaining their own systems. The NFT mechanism provides transparent ownership, enables secondary market trading of subscriptions, and creates a clear on-chain record of infrastructure access rights—all while maintaining the security isolation and performance characteristics required for production applications.

3. Customer Discovery Plan & Execution

Target Customer Segments

We identified our primary customer segment as blockchain development teams actively building decentralized applications who:

  • Operate their own blockchain observer infrastructure or frequently poll smart contracts
  • Require real-time responsiveness to on-chain events (e.g., DeFi protocols, gaming platforms, NFT marketplaces)
  • Face infrastructure cost pressures or operational complexity challenges
  • Value reliability and have low tolerance for missed events or downtime

Discovery Channels & Outreach Strategy

Community Engagement

Active participation in MultiversX builder groups, Telegram developer communities, and Discord channels where blockchain developers discuss infrastructure challenges and share technical solutions.

Direct Outreach

Personal connections with existing infrastructure partners and development teams we collaborated with on previous projects, leveraging established relationships to gather candid feedback.

Interview Methodology

We conducted customer discovery through a combination of asynchronous written interviews (via Telegram and Discord) and follow-up voice calls where deeper discussion was valuable. Our approach prioritized validating the problem severity before presenting our solution.

Core Interview Questions
  1. 1. Event Monitoring Patterns: "How frequently does your application react to blockchain events? What level of downtime or delayed detection is acceptable for your use case?"
  2. 2. Infrastructure Assessment: "What infrastructure components are you currently managing to monitor blockchain events? (nodes, proxies, queues, databases)"
  3. 3. Resilience Requirements: "How do you handle message replay when your clients disconnect? What historical buffer duration do you maintain?"
  4. 4. Access Control: "Would blockchain-based entitlement management (NFT-gated access) meet your security and governance requirements?"
  5. 5. Pricing Expectations: "What would you be willing to pay for production-grade, SLA-backed blockchain event delivery that eliminates your infrastructure maintenance burden?"

Discovery Timeline & Sample Size

Discovery interviews were conducted over a two-week period, engaging with 8 blockchain development teams and individual developers across various project types (DeFi, gaming, infrastructure tooling). The sample included both teams already operating their own observer infrastructure and teams exploring solutions to reduce their reliance on polling smart contracts.

4. Customer Discovery Process & Findings

Interview Process

Our discovery process followed a structured yet flexible approach designed to uncover genuine customer pain points before introducing our solution:

  1. 1
    Problem Exploration Phase:

    Initial conversations focused exclusively on understanding current infrastructure approaches and pain points. We asked open-ended questions about monitoring strategies, costs, and operational challenges without mentioning BeaconX.

  2. 2
    Solution Validation Phase:

    Only after confirming infrastructure challenges did we introduce the NFT-gated shared observer concept, observing reactions and gathering feedback on the approach.

  3. 3
    Practical Testing:

    We provided early access to our devnet deployment, allowing developers to test the actual product and provide feedback based on hands-on experience rather than hypothetical scenarios.

  4. 4
    Follow-up Iteration:

    After initial testing, we conducted follow-up conversations to understand usage patterns, technical questions, and feature requests that emerged from real-world integration attempts.

Key Discovery Findings

Finding #1: Universal Infrastructure Pain

Every developer interviewed confirmed experiencing infrastructure challenges. Teams running their own observers cited high server costs and maintenance burden, while teams polling smart contracts expressed frustration with rate limiting and delayed event detection. The problem validation was unanimous across all customer segments.

Finding #2: Replay Buffers as Critical Differentiator

Historical message replay emerged as a high-value feature. Developers emphasized that maintaining event history for debugging and handling client reconnections is complex and resource-intensive. Our automatic replay buffer capability directly addresses this pain point and differentiates BeaconX from basic API solutions.

Finding #3: NFT Entitlement Acceptance

Blockchain-native access control resonated with target customers. Developers understood the benefits of on-chain entitlement management, particularly for team credential rotation and transparent audit trails. The NFT mechanism was seen as innovative and appropriate for blockchain-focused development teams.

Finding #4: Network Coverage as Growth Driver

Demand exists across multiple blockchain networks. While initial deployment focused on MultiversX, developers explicitly requested testnet support and expansion to additional blockchain networks. This validates the scalability potential of the business model.

Finding #5: Price Sensitivity Varies by Team Size

Pricing expectations differed based on team maturity. Well-funded teams showed willingness to pay premium prices for reliability and SLA guarantees, while bootstrapped projects prioritized cost efficiency. This suggests tiered pricing aligned with usage and reliability requirements may be optimal.

Product Impact & Iteration

The discovery process validated our core product hypothesis while surfacing refinements to our roadmap:

  • Maintained: Core NFT-gated queue architecture remained unchanged, confirming the solution model is sound
  • Prioritized: Testnet support and expanded blockchain network coverage moved higher in development priority
  • Enhanced: Replay buffer retention policies and documentation became roadmap focus areas
  • Explored: Tiered pricing strategy to accommodate different team sizes and reliability requirements

6. Customer Validation Readiness Assessment

Based on the customer discovery findings, evidence of active usage, and commercial interest signals, we evaluate whether BeaconX is ready to progress to the Customer Validation stage focused on testing actual sales and revenue generation.

Assessment: Ready to Proceed with Customer Validation

We have successfully completed customer discovery and are prepared to move into customer validation. The evidence demonstrates clear progression beyond problem validation into solution adoption and early commercial interest. Our assessment is based on the following factors:

1. Problem Validation Complete

100% of interviewed developers confirmed experiencing the infrastructure pain points BeaconX addresses. The problem is real, widespread, and severe enough to drive behavior change. Teams are actively seeking alternatives to their current approaches.

2. Solution-Problem Fit Demonstrated

Developers not only understood the NFT-gated infrastructure concept but actively integrated it into their projects. Real production deployments (e.g., game development, DeFi applications) prove the solution effectively addresses customer needs. The technical architecture resonates with the blockchain-native target audience.

3. Paying Customer Signals Present

Multiple customers asked about mainnet availability, pricing structures, and commercial terms—clear indicators of purchase intent. The valoro.fund inquiry represents potential enterprise-level adoption. These conversations demonstrate progression beyond free tier curiosity to willingness to pay for production services.

4. Product-Market Fit Indicators

Unsolicited positive feedback ("you made my weekend"), requests for expanded network coverage, and infrastructure partnership discussions all indicate strong product-market fit signals. The solution is differentiated enough that customers view it as superior to building internal alternatives.

5. Technical Infrastructure Operational

BeaconX infrastructure is already deployed and operational on devnet with active users. The technical foundation (observers, queues, NFT entitlement backend, API middleware) is proven and scalable. We have de-risked core technical challenges before attempting to scale customer acquisition.

Next Steps for Customer Validation Phase

Moving into customer validation, our focus shifts from learning whether customers want the product to proving we can sell it repeatedly and profitably. Key activities include:

  1. 1. Mainnet Launch: Deploy production infrastructure and NFT contracts to mainnet to enable paying customers
  2. 2. Pricing Validation: Test pricing tiers with early customers to validate willingness to pay at proposed price points
  3. 3. Testnet Expansion: Roll out testnet support to address explicit customer requests and expand addressable use cases
  4. 4. Sales Process Definition: Document and refine the sales funnel from initial contact through NFT purchase and activation
  5. 5. Customer Success Framework: Establish onboarding, documentation, and support processes to ensure customer retention and satisfaction
  6. 6. Unit Economics Validation: Measure customer acquisition cost (CAC), lifetime value (LTV), and gross margins to prove business model viability
Conclusion

The customer discovery phase has provided strong validation that BeaconX solves a real, painful problem for blockchain development teams. Active usage in production environments, explicit commercial interest, and positive customer feedback collectively demonstrate readiness to progress into customer validation. We have moved beyond hypothesis testing into proving we can build a repeatable, scalable sales model around this validated product-market fit.
